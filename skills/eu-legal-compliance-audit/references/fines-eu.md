# Sanction reference — EU-wide and other member states

Last verified: 2026-10-18. Use this file when the owner is not established in Spain, or to add EU-level context. EU regulations (GDPR, AI Act, DSA) set the same ceilings everywhere; directives (ePrivacy/cookies, consumer law, accessibility) are transposed nationally, so their fine amounts **differ per country** and must be looked up.

## EU-wide ceilings (regulations — identical in every member state)

| Key | Law | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| `GDPR-*` (Art. 83(5)): principles, legal basis, consent, transparency, rights, transfers | GDPR | €20,000,000 or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher |
| `GDPR-*` (Art. 83(4)): minors' consent, privacy by design, processors, security, DPIA, DPO | GDPR | €10,000,000 or 2%, whichever is higher |
| `AIACT-5` prohibited practices | AI Act Art. 99(3) | €35,000,000 or 7% — for SMEs/start-ups, whichever is **lower** |
| `AIACT-50` and other operator duties | AI Act Art. 99(4) | €15,000,000 or 3% — SMEs: lower of the two |
| Incorrect information to authorities | AI Act Art. 99(5) | €7,500,000 or 1% — SMEs: lower |
| `DSA` | DSA Art. 52 | Up to 6% of worldwide annual turnover (national rules fix the actual scale) |

GDPR fines in practice depend heavily on the national DPA: some (Spain, Italy) fine small businesses often and modestly; others (Ireland, Netherlands) concentrate on fewer, larger cases. Use national observed practice when available.

## National regimes for transposed rules

Fill these per country with web search when available. Search queries that work: `"<country> cookie consent fine law article"`, `"<DPA name> sanction cookies 2026"`, `"<country> consumer protection fines amounts"`, `"<country> European Accessibility Act transposition penalties"`, `"<country> transposition directive 2023/2673 withdrawal button"`.

| Country | DPA / cookie authority | Cookies law | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| France | CNIL | Loi Informatique et Libertés Art. 82 | Very active on cookies (reject button, prior blocking); multi-million fines on large players, also simplified procedure with smaller fines for SMEs. Some audience-measurement cookies exempt if configured per CNIL guidance |
| Germany | Federal BfDI + 16 state DPAs | TDDDG §25 (formerly TTDSG) | §25 breaches fineable up to €300,000. Distinctive risk: **Abmahnungen** (cease-and-desist letters from competitors/associations) with lawyer-fee demands; Impressum duty under DDG §5 |
| Italy | Garante | Codice Privacy + Garante cookie guidelines (2021) | Frequent GDPR fines, including on SMEs |
| Netherlands | Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (privacy); ACM (cookies for some aspects) | Telecommunicatiewet 11.7a | Active cookie sweeps |
| Ireland | DPC (privacy); cookies also DPC | S.I. 336/2011 | Focus on large tech; few SME fines |
| Portugal | CNPD | Lei 41/2004 | — |
| Belgium | APD/GBA | Law of 13 June 2005 | Strict line on cookie walls and reject buttons |

For any country not listed: identify the DPA from the EDPB members list, then search its sanctions.

## Non-EU users (brief)

- UK: UK GDPR + PECR (cookies) — ICO; PECR fines raised by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 to GDPR-level ceilings (verify current status).
- Switzerland: revFADP — fines on responsible individuals, up to CHF 250,000.
- A non-EU company targeting EU users is still subject to GDPR (Art. 3(2)) and must appoint an EU representative (Art. 27) unless exempt.
